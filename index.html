Zen Phone Lock — Privacy Policy

Package: com.zenphonelock.app

Last updated: 14 September 2026

No data collection

Zen Focus Lock has no account, no analytics, no ad network, and sends no data anywhere. The one exception is a read-only check of whether your device currently has a network connection at all (Wi-Fi/cellular up or down) — used only to require being online before the "watch ads to unlock early" option can be used, so it can't be bypassed via airplane mode; this check contacts nothing and transmits nothing itself. Every setting and session record it keeps — your duration, allowed apps, and session history — is stored only in this app's private local storage on your device. Nothing is ever sent anywhere, and nothing is shared with any third party.

What the Accessibility permission is used for

Zen Focus Lock uses Android's Accessibility service mainly to detect which app is in the foreground, so it can show a restriction screen in place of apps you didn't allow during an active Zen Lock session, or a Zen Balance app once it's gone over its own time limit (or is set to always-block) — this only needs window-change events, not screen content. It also periodically checks for, and detects interaction with, a floating (Picture-in-Picture) window belonging to any app currently blocked this way. Rather than re-locking your screen the instant such a window is seen, it first tries clicking that window's own close button automatically for up to a few seconds — only if the window is still open after that does it return you to the lock screen. It also detects when YouTube's Shorts feed specifically is open, so Shorts can stay locked during Zen Lock even when you've allowed YouTube itself — done by matching YouTube's own internal screen-element names for its Shorts player, not by reading any video, comment, or account content. These are the only two cases where it reads window content — not screen pixels — and only for that floating window or the foreground YouTube window; it never reads the content of any other app's screen, never reads form contents or typed text, and never collects, stores, or transmits anything it reads.

There is one further, narrower case, used only as a last resort: if a floating YouTube window can't be classified as Shorts or not by either of the checks above, the app takes a single on-device screenshot (Android 11 and newer only) to check whether that window's video is a vertical video shown pillarboxed with letterbox-style bars — nothing more specific than that shape is examined. The screenshot is cropped to that one floating window, analyzed in-memory, and discarded immediately; it is never saved to storage, never displayed anywhere, and never transmitted off your device. This uses Android's on-device accessibility screenshot API, not screen recording or casting, and requires no permission beyond the Accessibility access already described here.

How call state is detected

Zen Focus Lock listens for the system's call-state broadcast to detect whether a call is ringing or active, so it can guarantee it never blocks or interferes with incoming calls or the in-call screen. This does not require the READ_PHONE_STATE permission and never accesses your phone number, call log, or contacts.

What the Usage Access permission is used for

Zen Balance (and the Awareness screen) use Android's Usage Access permission to read how many minutes each app has been on screen — nothing else. The Awareness screen can show this for today, the past week, or the past month, but in every case it's only ever screen-on time, never what's inside an app. Usage totals are computed on the fly from Android's own records and are not stored or transmitted anywhere by this app.

Audio during Zen Balance

When a Zen Balance app goes over its session limit, this app claims audio focus to stop that app's music or video from continuing to play in the background — the same standard Android mechanism any media app already listens for (e.g. a phone call interrupting music). This only affects playback in the exact moment a limit is reached; audio focus is released as soon as the intervention screen closes.

How a hard block stays enforced

Whenever a session hard-blocks something — a disallowed app during Zen Focus Lock, Settings/Play Store/the uninstall screen, or Zen Balance's over-limit stop — enforcement relies on the device lock alone, not on Android's "Screen Pinning" feature. If you use Home, Recents, or Back to reach whatever was blocked, this app detects it reappearing and re-blocks it (re-locking your screen if needed) the same way every time, rather than trying to prevent you from navigating there at all.

Optional floating status bubble

If you separately grant "Display over other apps" (Android's own special permission, not a normal one — it's granted from Settings, and Zen Focus Lock never assumes it's on), a small floating bubble can appear over a blocked app's Picture-in-Picture window (e.g. a YouTube Short during Zen Lock, or any app's PiP window once it's over its Zen Balance limit) showing your remaining time, with a tap to return to the app. This is entirely optional and purely informational — it has no enforcement power of its own, and every restriction still works fully whether or not you grant it.

"Turn off floating video" shortcut

The first time a restricted app's Picture-in-Picture video is detected, the lock screen offers a one-time button that opens that app's own Picture-in-Picture setting in Android Settings — this only opens the screen; it cannot change that app's settings for you, only you can, by confirming it there yourself. Zen Focus Lock has no partnership or affiliation with any app named here (e.g. YouTube) — it's just naming which app's settings screen the button opens.

Payment apps, outgoing calls, social media, games, entertainment, shopping, and food delivery

During Zen Lock or Complete Lock, payment apps, outgoing calls (via your device's default Phone app), social media apps, games, entertainment apps (Netflix, Prime Video, Disney+ Hotstar, Pinterest, and other video-streaming/OTT apps), shopping apps, food delivery apps, and quick-commerce/grocery apps are always restricted — there's no way to allow any of these, even individually. Web browsers and YouTube are handled differently: under Zen Focus Lock, either can be added to your allow-list and opened like any other app (YouTube's Shorts feed specifically stays locked either way — see "What the Accessibility permission is used for" above); under Complete Lock, everything non-essential is restricted regardless, so browsers and YouTube are blocked there too, the same as everything else outside its fixed essentials list. Zen Focus Lock only controls whether these apps can be opened; it never reads, stores, or otherwise interacts with any payment credentials, PINs, OTPs, transactions, social media content, game progress, purchases, orders, or browsing history.

What always stays available

Incoming calls, emergency calling, your Camera app, and any Calendar, Calculator, or Clock app are always reachable, regardless of what's running or which mode is active.

Settings, Play Store, and uninstalling during a session

While any session (Lock, Complete Lock, or Balance) is active, this app also blocks Android Settings, the Google Play Store app, and the system uninstall confirmation screen — the same unconditional way described above, in every mode. This exists so a session can't be undone from the outside (disabling Accessibility/Device Admin, or removing the app entirely) partway through. You can always end a session — either by letting the timer finish on its own (free and instant), or through the watch-ads/pay early-exit option — and once no session is active, Settings, Play Store, and uninstalling are all immediately reachable again with no restriction.

Device Admin

Starting your first session asks you to activate "Device admin" status for this app, which is the only way a normal (non-enterprise) app can make itself harder to uninstall — Android requires a device admin to be deactivated first, before it can be removed. This app requests exactly one device-management policy with it: force-lock (no data wipe, no camera disable, nothing else) — used whenever a session blocks something (a disallowed app, Settings, Play Store, the uninstall screen, or Zen Balance's over-limit stop), which locks your screen immediately; the block screen itself only appears once you unlock the device yourself, so it never sits behind or interferes with your own lock-screen credential prompt. force-lock only ever triggers your own existing lock-screen credential (PIN/pattern/biometric) — this app never sets, sees, or changes that credential. Device admin status, once granted, stays active permanently rather than being turned off between sessions, so you're only asked to activate it the one time rather than before every session — the tradeoff being that uninstalling always requires deactivating it first, session or not. You can deactivate it yourself at any time from Android Settings → Security → Device admin apps — that path can never be blocked by this or any app, it's an OS-level guarantee.

Ads and payments to end a session early

The "watch ads" and "pay to unlock" options shown when ending a session early are self-imposed friction, not a discount or reward — they exist to make quitting early cost some effort. The ad option is a placeholder in this build: no ad network is integrated yet. The payment option is processed entirely through Google Play Billing — this app never sees or stores your card, UPI, or account details; Google handles the transaction and the app only learns whether it succeeded. Letting the timer finish on its own is always free and instant.

Temporary "watch an ad" unlock

During any active session, watching one placeholder ad unlocks every app for 5 minutes without ending the session — capped at 2 uses per rolling hour so it can't be chained to defeat the session entirely. Like the early-exit ad option, no real ad network is integrated in this build yet.

Zen Premium subscription

Zen Premium is a recurring subscription (monthly or yearly) processed entirely through Google Play Billing — this app never sees or stores your card, UPI, or account details. Prices are set and localized to your country by Google Play. Premium removes the ad requirement from the two unlock mechanics above (still capped the same as for everyone else) — it does not change what data this app collects, since it collects none. Manage or cancel your subscription any time from Google Play's own subscriptions page.

Known limitations

Outgoing-call restriction only applies to your device's default Phone app; calls placed from other apps (e.g. VoIP or messaging apps) cannot be blocked without enterprise device-management enrollment, which this app does not use. Camera access is allowed at the whole-app level only — it cannot be limited to just photo-taking, since doing so would require reading on-screen content, which this app deliberately never does. Nothing in this app can suppress the notification shade — a determined person can still swipe it down and jump elsewhere from a notification, and this app will simply re-block whatever they land on the same way it always does — since fully preventing that would require "Device Owner" provisioning, which can only be granted before any Google account is added to a device (in practice, a factory reset) and which this app deliberately doesn't require.
